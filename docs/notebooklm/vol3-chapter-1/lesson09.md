**GRAMMAR_RULE**

**اِسْمُ الفَاعِلِ (The Active Participle)**
The Active Participle (Ism Al-Fael) is a noun derived from a verb that indicates the "doer" of the action. Like other nouns and adjectives in Arabic, it must match the noun it describes in gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/dual/plural) [1]. 

**MASDAR_FACTORY**

**Base Form (Form I / مُجَرَّد):**
Pattern for Masculine: **فَاعِلٌ** (Singular) - **فَاعِلَانِ** (Dual) - **فَاعِلُونَ** (Plural) [1]
Pattern for Feminine: **فَاعِلَةٌ** (Singular) - **فَاعِلَتَانِ** (Dual) - **فَاعِلَاتٌ** (Plural) [1]

*Example using **النَّصْرُ** (To help):*
*   **نَاصِرٌ** - **نَاصِرَانِ** - **نَاصِرُونَ** (A male helper - Two male helpers - Many male helpers) [1]
*   **نَاصِرَةٌ** - **نَاصِرَتَانِ** - **نَاصِرَاتٌ** (A female helper - Two female helpers - Many female helpers) [1]

**GRAMMAR_RULE**

**Ism Fael used as الحَال (Condition / State)**
When an Ism Fael is used in the accusative case (Mansub / ending with Fathatain like قَائِمًا or جَالِسًا), it functions as an adverb of state (حَال - Haal). It describes the physical state or condition of the subject while the action is taking place [2]. 
*Example:* **قَائِمًا** means "in a standing state", and **جَالِسًا** means "in a sitting state" [2].
