### GRAMMAR_RULE
**The Circumstantial Qualifier (الْحَال - Al-Haal)**
The *Haal* describes the physical or figurative condition/state of the subject (فَاعِل) or the object (مَفْعُول بِهِ) at the time an action takes place [3, 4].

**Rule 1: Haal as a Single Word (Mufrad)**
When the *Haal* is a single word, it is usually derived from the Active Participle (اِسْمُ الْفَاعِل). It must match the noun it describes in gender and number, but it is **always in the accusative state (مَنْصُوب - Mansub)**.
*   Example: قَرَأَ رَاشِدٌ **جَالِسًا** (Rashid read *sitting/while sitting*).

**Rule 2: Haal as a Nominal Sentence (Jumla Ismiyyah) with وَ (Waw Al-Haal)**
The *Haal* can also be an entire sentence attached to the main clause using the "Waw of Haal" (وَاوُ الْحَال), which translates to "while". It is usually followed by a separate pronoun.
*   Example: قَرَأَ رَاشِدٌ **وَهُوَ جَالِسٌ** (Rashid read *while he was sitting*).

### APPLICATION
**Form 1: Haal as an Accusative Single Word (Ism Fa'il) [3]**
*   قَرَأَ رَاشِدٌ جَالِسًا (عَلَى الْكُرْسِيِّ).
    *Rashid read sitting (on the chair).*
*   قَرَأَ الْأَوْلَادُ جَالِسِينَ.
    *The boys read sitting.*
*   قَرَأَتِ الْبِنْتُ جَالِسَةً.
    *The girl read sitting.*
*   قَرَأَتِ الْبَنَاتُ جَالِسَاتٍ. *(Note: Plural feminine takes Kasrah in Mansub state)*
    *The girls read sitting.*
*   قَضَى حَيَاتَهُ مُجَاهِدًا فِي سَبِيلِ اللهِ.
    *He spent his life struggling in the path of Allah.*
*   قَضَوْا حَيَاتَهُمْ مُجَاهِدِينَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللهِ.
    *They spent their lives struggling in the path of Allah.*
*   خَرَجَ الْوَلَدُ مِنَ الْبَيْتِ مُسَلِّمًا عَلَى أَبِيهِ.
    *The boy left the house greeting his father.*
*   خَرَجَتِ الْبَنَاتُ مُسَلِّمَاتٍ عَلَى أُمَّهَاتِهِنَّ.
    *The girls left greeting their mothers.*
*   رَأَيْتُكَ فِي الْمَسْجِدِ مُصَلِّيًا.
    *I saw you in the mosque praying.*

**Form 2 & 3: Haal as a Clause with Waw Al-Haal (وَاوُ الْحَال) [3, 4]**
*   قَرَأَ رَاشِدٌ وَهُوَ جَالِسٌ (عَلَى الْكُرْسِيِّ).
    *Rashid read while he was sitting (on the chair).*
*   قَرَأَ الْأَوْلَادُ وَهُمْ جَالِسُونَ.
    *The boys read while they were sitting.*
*   قَرَأَتِ الْبِنْتُ وَهِيَ جَالِسَةٌ.
    *The girl read while she was sitting.*
*   قَضَتْ حَيَاتَهَا وَهِيَ تُجَاهِدُ...
    *She spent her life while she was struggling...*
*   خَرَجَ الْأَوْلَادُ مِنَ الْبَيْتِ وَهُمْ يُسَلِّمُونَ...
    *The boys left the house while they were greeting...*
*   رَأَيْتُكَ فِي الْمَسْجِدِ وَأَنْتَ تُصَلِّي.
    *I saw you in the mosque while you were praying.*
*   رَأَيْتُكُنَّ فِي الْبَيْتِ وَأَنْتُنَّ تُصَلِّينَ.
    *I saw you (fem. pl.) in the house while you were praying.*

### QA
**Q:** Why does the word جَالِسَات change to جَالِسَاتٍ (with a Kasrah instead of a Fatha) in the sentence "قَرَأَتِ الْبَنَاتُ جَالِسَاتٍ"?
**A:** The word is in the position of *Haal*, which must be in the accusative state (مَنْصُوب). However, "جَالِسَات" is a sound feminine plural (جَمْع مُؤَنَّث سَالِم), and the rule dictates that sound feminine plurals take a *Kasrah* in place of a *Fatha* when they are in the accusative state.

**Q:** What is the difference between "قَرَأَ رَاشِدٌ جَالِسًا" and "قَرَأَ رَاشِدٌ وَهُوَ جَالِسٌ"?
**A:** Grammatically, in the first sentence, "جَالِسًا" is a single-word *Haal* in the accusative state. In the second sentence, "وَهُوَ جَالِسٌ" is a nominal sentence (Jumla Ismiyyah) acting collectively as the *Haal* clause, connected by the *Waw Al-Haal*. Both translate effectively to the same meaning: "Rashid read while sitting."
