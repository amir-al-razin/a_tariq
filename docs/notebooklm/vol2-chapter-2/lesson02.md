### GRAMMAR_RULE
**جُمْلَةٌ يَقَعُ فِيْهَا صِفَةٌ لِاسْمٍ قَبْلَهَا (A Sentence Acting as an Adjective)** [3]
This rule explains that when a full sentence immediately follows an indefinite noun (*Nakirah*), the entire sentence functions as an adjective (*Sifah*) describing that noun. The Arabic rule is often summarized as: الجُمَلُ بَعْدَ النَّكِرَاتِ صِفَاتٌ (Sentences after indefinite nouns are adjectives).

**Examples applying the rule:**
*   جَاءَنِي رَجُلٌ (A man came to me) + يَعْرِفُ اللُغَةَ العَرَبِيَّةَ (He knows the Arabic language)
    *   **Combined:** جَاءَنِي رَجُلٌ يَعْرِفُ اللُغَةَ العَرَبِيَّةَ (A man who knows the Arabic language came to me) [3].
*   هَذَا عَمَلٌ (This is a deed) + لَا يُضِيعُ أَجْرَهُ إِنْ شَاءَ اللهُ (Its reward will not be lost, God willing)
    *   **Combined:** هَذَا عَمَلٌ لَا يُضِيعُ أَجْرَهُ إِنْ شَاءَ اللهُ (This is a deed whose reward will not be lost, God willing) [3].
*   أَلْبَسَ ثَوْبًا لَوْنُهُ جَمِيلٌ (I am wearing a garment whose color is beautiful) [3].
*   اِقْرَأْ كِتَابًا يَنْفَعُكَ فِي دِينِكَ (Read a book that benefits you in your religion) [3].
