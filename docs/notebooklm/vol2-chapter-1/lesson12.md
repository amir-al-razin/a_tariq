### **Reading Passages**
The reading passages demonstrate how sentences change when **كَانَ**, **صَارَ**, and **لَيْسَ** are applied. Notice that the predicate (the describing word) changes to end with a *Fathatayn* ( ً ) [1, 2].

**Third Person (He/She) [1]:**
*   رَاشِدٌ تِلْمِيْذٌ (Rashid is a student.)
*   كَانَ رَاشِدٌ تِلْمِيْذًا (Rashid was a student.)
*   صَارَ رَاشِدٌ مُعَلِّمًا (Rashid became a teacher.)
*   لَيْسَ رَاشِدٌ تِلْمِيْذًا (Rashid is not a student.)
*   فَاطِمَةُ صَغِيْرَةٌ (Fatima is young.)
*   كَانَتْ فَاطِمَةُ صَغِيْرَةً (Fatima was young.)
*   صَارَتْ فَاطِمَةُ كَبِيْرَةً (Fatima became old.)
*   لَيْسَتْ فَاطِمَةُ صَغِيْرَةً (Fatima is not young.)

### **Grammar Extension: Using "بِ" with "لَيْسَ"**
The second half of the reading demonstrates an alternate, more emphatic way to structure sentences with **لَيْسَ** by attaching the letter **بِ** to the predicate. This changes the end of the word to take a *Kasratayn* ( ٍ ) instead of a *Fathatayn* ( ً ) [2]. 

*   لَيْسَ رَاشِدٌ كَاذِبًا ⟵ لَيْسَ رَاشِدٌ بِكَاذِبٍ (Rashid is *not* a liar.)
*   لَسْتَ صَادِقًا ⟵ لَسْتَ بِصَادِقٍ (You are *not* truthful.)
*   لَسْتِ مُتَكَبِّرَةً ⟵ لَسْتِ بِمُتَكَبِّرَةٍ (You are *not* arrogant.)
*   لَسْتُ مُنَافِقًا ⟵ لَسْتُ بِمُنَافِقٍ (I am *not* a hypocrite.)
*   لَيْسَتِ الحُجْرَةُ بِوَاسِعَةٍ (The room is *not* spacious.)
